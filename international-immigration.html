Sep 11, 2026

Canada: UK professionals eligible for LMIA-exempt work permits under CPTPP

Effective September 1, UK nationals qualify for LMIA-exempt Canadian work permits under the CPTPP International Mobility Program's Professionals category, now in force between the UK and Canada — a new route alongside existing IEC and CUKTCA options. BAL Immigration News

Sep 11, 2026

UK lays autumn 2026 immigration rule changes before Parliament

Statement of Changes HC 584 introduces targeted amendments to Skilled Worker (modern-slavery protections), Student, EU Settlement Scheme, Hong Kong BN(O), and visitor routes. Most changes take effect October 8, 2026; the student-route maintenance requirement rises on November 30. Gherson

2026

Germany plans "Work-and-Stay Agency" to fast-track skilled worker visas

Germany will centralise visa and residence-permit procedures for foreign professionals on a single digital platform. Between June 2024 and November 2025, German missions issued 17,489 "opportunity cards" for job seekers plus 838 experience-pillar visas — while experts estimate hundreds of thousands of new skilled workers are needed each year. European Commission

2026

EU unveils new migration and visa strategy to attract global talent

The European Commission's strategy pairs tighter border security with talent attraction: new Talent Partnerships, faster recognition of foreign qualifications, digital visa procedures, and longer-validity multiple-entry visas for trusted travellers. European Commission

Reference

EU Blue Card: revised rules for highly skilled migrant workers

The revised Blue Card Directive brings more flexible qualification requirements, easier employer changes, family reunification with labour-market access, and greater mobility between EU countries for highly qualified non-EU nationals. European Migration Network

Reference

Europe adjusts labour migration to fill shortages

Countries from Croatia to Romania raised migrant-worker quotas for tourism, agriculture and construction; Belgium dropped labour-market tests for shortage sectors; Austria extended its Red-White-Red Card; Romania added a digital-nomad visa. Eurofound

Key takeaway: While some destinations tighten, the EU is actively competing for skilled migrants — Blue Card reforms, opportunity cards and talent partnerships are opening new legal pathways.

🎓 Educational Visas

Sep 7, 2026

Canada tightens study-permit financial rules and anti-fraud scrutiny

Effective September 1, 2026, a single study-permit applicant outside Quebec must show at least C$22,895 in living expenses, alongside reduced intake targets and a centralized letter-of-acceptance verification platform by September 30. Co-op work placements no longer need a separate work permit; PGWP rules stay unchanged for 2026. Higher Education Plus

Jul 2026

Australia hikes student visa fee to AU$2,500 — up 285% since 2022

Australia's student visa application fee now dwarfs equivalents: ~AU$775 in the US, ~AU$935 in the UK, ~AU$240 in Canada, ~AU$790 in New Zealand. The sector calls it "a direct hit to Australia's competitiveness." ICEF Monitor

May 2026

UK sponsored study visas down 32%; rejection rates surge

The Home Office issued 32% fewer sponsored study visas in Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025. Refusal rates for students from key growth markets doubled or tripled — Pakistan's rejection rate jumped nearly six-fold, from under 6% to 41%. ICEF Monitor

2026

UK Graduate Route cut to 18 months from January 2027

Britain's post-study work window shrinks from two years to 18 months starting January 1, 2027, and the UK now ranks last in student sentiment for post-study work among major destinations. Canada's up-to-3-year post-graduation work offer stands out by contrast. VisaVerge

May 2026

Students pivot to Asia and Europe as Big Four tighten

Through Q1 2026, restrictive settings in Australia, Canada, the UK and the US continued reducing new foreign-student inflows — while boosting interest in Asian and European destinations and institutions. ICEF Monitor

Key takeaway: Compare total costs and post-study work rights across destinations before applying — fees, refusal rates and work rights now vary dramatically between the US, UK, Canada, Australia and emerging alternatives like Germany.
