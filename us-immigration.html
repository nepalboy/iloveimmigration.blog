⚖️ Legal Migration

Sep 11, 2026

Trump administration proposes ending the 60-day grace period for H-1B workers after job loss

DHS published a proposed rule that would eliminate the discretionary 60-day grace period for H-1B, L-1, O-1, E-1/E-2/E-3, H-1B1 and TN workers whose employment ends — plus their dependents. Laid-off workers would fall out of status the day employment ends, while the current rule stays in effect until a final rule is issued. USA Today

Sep 11, 2026

USCIS resumes FY2026 Diversity Visa adjudications after federal court order

Following a district court order in Medani v. Trump, USCIS is resuming adjudication of certain pending adjustment-of-status applications under the FY2026 Diversity Visa program — a rare opening for DV selectees. Green Card Maker

Sep 12, 2026

Green cards for couples are slowing down as visa interviews pause worldwide

Family-based green card petitions are stalling: the State Department paused immigrant visa interviews at embassies and consulates globally to train staff on a new applicant-screening policy. Couples — including those from countries hit by an earlier immigrant-visa pause covering 75 nations — face uncertain timelines. USA Today

Sep 9, 2026

US resumes immigrant visa processing for Hungary and Poland; pause remains elsewhere

Immigrant visa processing restarted in Hungary and Poland, but the broader suspension affecting dozens of countries continues, alongside expanded social-media vetting for applicants. Reuters

Sep 9, 2026

El Salvador TPS holders keep protected status for now

Some 170,000 Salvadoran TPS holders — about 152,000 in the US workforce — retain protection while DHS weighs its decision, after TPS was ended for nationals of several other countries. Reuters

Jul 2026

New public-charge rule makes green cards harder for some applicants

A revived rule lets officers weigh a wider set of means-tested public benefits — and benefits used by family members — when deciding green card applications. It takes effect September 18. CNN

Jun 2026

Legal immigration approvals fell 27% in 2025, data shows

USCIS approved 8.3 million applications in 2025 vs 11.4 million in 2024. Employment-based petitions dropped 26%, humanitarian 69%, green-card approvals 16% — while family-based approvals rose 8%. USA Today

Key takeaway: Legal pathways are tightening on multiple fronts — longer green-card waits, expanded vetting, and stricter public-charge screening. Applicants should file early and track policy changes closely.

🎓 Educational Visas

Sep 11, 2026

New F-1 rules take effect: fixed stays, no first-year major changes

Under the new framework, undergraduates are limited to 4 years and master's students to their program's exact duration. Students needing more time must apply to USCIS for an extension; first-year major changes and transfers are restricted; off-campus work authorization now requires it to be a graduation requirement. WAMC

Aug 2026

US issued 23% fewer student visas in 2025; F-1 down by a third

New F-1, M-1 and J-1 issuances fell sharply — India down 57%, China 28%, Nepal 55%, Ghana 52%. Since many students later transition to H-1B work visas and green cards, experts warn the drop will cascade for years. ICEF Monitor

Jul 2026

DHS finalizes end of "duration of status" for F and J visas

The final rule caps admission at the program length (max 4 years), requires USCIS extension applications with biometric vetting, cuts the post-OPT grace period from 60 to 30 days, and restricts Day-1 CPT for second degrees at the same level. Dept. of Homeland Security

Key takeaway: The F-1 route is narrower and costlier than a year ago — plan for fixed timelines, earlier job/sponsorship planning, and possible extension filings.
